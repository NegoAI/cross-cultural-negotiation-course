## Question 1 - Interests & BATNAs (29/30)

This is an excellent answer. Both sides' interests are identified, explicitly ranked, and explained through case evidence rather than asserted. The analysis consistently looks behind positions to interests: the reading of the 70-30 equity dispute as a question of autonomy and governance rather than percentages, and the observation that technology transfer serves opposite interests for each side, are exactly the level this question asks for. Both BATNAs are named specifically (Cisco, Samsung, AT&T for Nora; the UK/EU option for Sakari), assessed for quality rather than just listed, and the payoff is stated in full: the BATNA asymmetry, combined with Nora's time pressure, explains who could afford to hold firm. The closing synthesis draws the practical guideline (build the BATNA before the negotiation, separate positions from interests early) and earns it.

What kept this from a 30: Sakari's documented concern about the efficiency and quality of the Malaysian workforce, one of the two main issues of the January meeting, does not appear in its interest set. It is the one place where the case offers an interest the answer leaves unused.

## Question 2 - Mr. Hashim's email to Mr. Kuusisto (27/30)

The communication-context call is correct and doubly grounded: Hall's framework for the low-context choice, and the behavioral dimension of Cultural Intelligence for why Hashim, specifically, must set aside his own high-context instincts. The email does the hard part of the task: it names the failed February negotiation, reframes the breakdown as a process problem rather than a personal or cultural failing (correctly identified as Fisher and Ury's separation of people from problem), and then addresses all five outstanding issues with a concrete mechanism for each: governance rights in exchange for the fixed 70-30 equity, a phased and milestone-based technology transfer, a performance-linked royalty (correctly recognized as a contingent contract), internationally benchmarked salaries with a localization plan, and a neutral arbitration seat in Singapore. Nothing is a placeholder. The reasoning section justifies each choice culturally rather than just describing it.

What kept this from the top band: reconciliation is the question's headline ask, and the email heals the rupture only in the abstract. The concrete break, the removal of Mr. Pekkarinen from the February negotiation, is never acknowledged, and no relational repair gesture (an offer to travel to Helsinki, a joint technical workshop) accompanies the issue proposals; the case itself frames Hashim's outreach as an act of reconciliation after that incident, so a complete answer meets it head on. Second, the question asks for reasoning on each sentence, and the answer reasons per paragraph and per choice instead. One small irony worth noticing: the email opens with "I hope this message finds you well," which is precisely the small-talk instinct the answer's own analysis says to set aside for a Finnish reader.

## Question 3 - Cultural dimensions analysis (28/30)

A very strong selection, and every dimension is attributed to its theorist correctly: Hall's high/low context, Trompenaars' specific/diffuse, universalism/particularism, and neutral/affective, and Hofstede's power distance. Power distance, the case's most powerful dimension, is here, with the right structural evidence: the leader-led January meeting against the stalled February meeting held without either principal. Each dimension is anchored in case facts and traced as a mechanism, with both directions of misreading shown (Finnish restraint read as coldness, Malaysian indirectness read as vagueness). The closing move is genuine critical thinking: bounding what culture can explain, distinguishing cultural amplification from the real divergence of strategic interests established in Question 1, and locating the actual gap in insufficient Cultural Intelligence on both sides.

Two points of softness. First, the high/low context entry and the neutral/affective entry rest partly on the same body of evidence, Finnish reserve; the mechanisms are distinguished correctly (how meaning is carried versus norms of emotional display), but a fifth slot with fully independent evidence, for example monochronic time against Nora's TMB deadline, would have made the selection airtight. Second, the power distance entry states that the February meeting stalled "precisely because" the remaining negotiators lacked the standing to move fixed positions; the leaders' absence is in the case, but that causal mechanism is the answer's own inference, and presenting an interpretation as established fact overstates what the case supports. The reading is plausible; the certainty is not earned.

## Question 4 - Joint venture & multicultural team building (27/30)

The strongest structural choice in this answer is presenting the roadmap first and embedding the tool in it, with the explicit argument that a tool delivered outside a process is a one-off training session. The four-phase roadmap is sensible and case-derived (the Pekkarinen episode and the silence misreading recur as training material rather than trivia). The CQ framework is given its actual mechanics: all four components are named, each mapped to specific phases and to specific failures from the negotiation, with concrete behaviors at the end (written follow-ups, confirming understanding before decisions, inviting quieter colleagues to speak). The opening script names the real tensions honestly, sets four habits and four principles, and states the third-culture goal plainly: one team culture drawing on the strengths of both.

What a top answer would have added: first, CQ is a capability framework rather than one of the course's team-building instruments; the strongest version either selects one of the multicultural tools from the toolset discussed in class or explicitly pairs CQ with one, showing what the team does together in the room. Second, a question entirely about team formation is the natural home of psychological safety, which never appears by name (trust as reliability comes close). Third, the script itself is delivered culture-neutrally; addressing a mixed Malaysian-Finnish room is itself a cross-cultural communication task, and a fully applied answer designs the delivery: explicit structure for the Finnish side, relational warmth for the Malaysian side. One factual caution, appearing twice in this answer and once in Question 3's conclusion: the claim that Sakari read Nora's indirectness as vagueness is a symmetry the case never documents; the case evidences only Nora's misreading of the Finns. Inventing the reciprocal misreading is a small embellishment, and in a case-based exam the case sets the boundary of what can be asserted.
